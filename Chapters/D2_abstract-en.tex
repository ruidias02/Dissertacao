The \gls{NDN} paradigm offers a promising alternative to traditional IP-based communication by focusing on data instead of host addresses. However, existing synchronization mechanisms such as \gls{SVSync} were originally designed for stable environments and do not fully exploit the dynamics of \glspl{VANETs}.

This work proposes a \textit{hierarchical} synchronization model based on \gls{SVSync}, tailored to the characteristics of vehicular \gls{V-NDN}. The architecture introduces three synchronization phases coordinated by distributed pivots, aiming to reduce message overhead, improve scalability, and accelerate the convergence of network state. The solution was implemented and evaluated using the \textit{ndnSIM} simulator, where multiple network densities were tested and metrics such as latency, synchronization traffic, packet losses, and scalability were analyzed.

Results show that the proposed hierarchical model significantly improves synchronization performance compared to non-hierarchical approaches, reducing message exchange and improving overall efficiency. These findings contribute to the development of more effective data dissemination and coordination mechanisms for vehicular networks based on \gls{NDN}.
